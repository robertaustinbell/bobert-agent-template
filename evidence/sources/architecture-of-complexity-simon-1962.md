# Simon, “The Architecture of Complexity” (1962)

## Provenance and coverage

A 17-page text-based PDF was inspected completely. Text extraction returned substantive text on every PDF page. PDF page numbers below refer to the inspected artifact; printed pages are 467–482.

## Source claims

Simon defines a nearly decomposable system as one in which interactions among subsystems are weak but not negligible relative to interactions within subsystems. In his room-temperature example, short-run behavior is approximately independent by room, while longer-run behavior depends on aggregate cross-room coupling (PDF pp. 9–11; printed pp. 474–476).

Near decomposability is temporal and approximate, not absolute: at least two characteristic timescales matter, with rapid within-subsystem adjustment and slower cross-subsystem effects. The usefulness of a subsystem boundary therefore depends on the horizon and question (PDF pp. 10–12; printed pp. 475–477).

Simon argues that aggregate descriptions can sometimes represent longer-run interactions without preserving every internal detail, while short-run prediction may require a finer description (PDF pp. 10–12; printed pp. 475–477).

He also warns that apparent hierarchical structure can partly reflect the observer's descriptive and cognitive limits rather than an intrinsic architecture of the system (PDF pp. 14–16; printed pp. 479–481).

## Agent-design synthesis

**Operational synthesis, not a procedure stated verbatim by Simon:** treat subsystem boundaries, decomposition, and aggregation as model claims. Ask whether within-boundary interactions are materially stronger or faster than cross-boundary interactions over the declared horizon, whether the selected level preserves the prediction target, and whether changing the boundary or aggregation changes the forecast or decision.

## Limits and rejected transfers

This template does not adopt:

- near decomposability from a diagram, module tree, organizational chart, topical outline, or convenient category;
- hierarchy as inherently efficient, natural, authoritative, purposive, inevitable, or desirable;
- Simon's watchmaker calculation or biological analogies as universal quantitative laws;
- subsystem independence beyond the horizon and coupling conditions that support it;
- aggregate variables without evidence that omitted detail cannot alter the target or decision;
- every discussion of evolution, entropy, genetics, cognition, or social organization as relevant to forecast doctrine.

The paper is a broad theoretical essay with illustrative models and analogies, not empirical proof that arbitrary human, organizational, market, or agent systems are nearly decomposable.

## Disposition

Bounded strengthening evidence for level-of-description and aggregation checks in `operating-thought/decisions/decision-quality-under-uncertainty.md`. No standalone hierarchy, decomposition, or complexity doctrine is justified.
